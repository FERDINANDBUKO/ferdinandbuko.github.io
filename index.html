  2. Type what you noticed, in your own words. Something like "the save button does nothing when I change my email" is perfect.
  3. Answer the short follow-up questions klipp asks. It only asks when something important is missing.
  4. Check the summary klipp shows you. It will say whether it thinks this is a bug, a feature request or a suggestion.
  5. Press send. The ticket appears on GitHub, and the tester gets a friendly confirmation.

Nothing gets lost, and nobody has to remember what they meant three days later.

🧠 How klipp Thinks About a Report

When a tester writes a note, klipp reads it the way a careful colleague would. It looks for the problem, the context and the expectation. Then it sorts the report into one of three buckets.

Bugs

Something that used to work and now does not, or something that behaves differently from what the app promises. klipp will want to know what the person did, what happened, and what they expected instead.

Feature requests

Something that does not exist yet but should. klipp will try to understand the goal behind the request, not just the wording, so the ticket describes the problem worth solving.

Suggestions

Small improvements to wording, layout or flow. These are easy to lose, so klipp captures them before they fade away.

If a report is unclear, klipp does not guess. It asks one or two plain questions and waits for the answer. That single habit is what turns a one-line complaint into a ticket a developer can actually act on.
